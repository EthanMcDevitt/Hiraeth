# Hiraeth

A storybook-style, turn-based fantasy journey built with Pygame. Hiraeth uses characters saved by the parent Character Workshop in `characters.json`; it saves the current journey to `hiraeth.json` in this folder.

From the workspace root, install the dependency and launch:

```powershell
py -m pip install -r hiraeth_pygame\requirements.txt
py hiraeth_pygame\main.py
```

Create characters from the opening menu with dropdowns for races and traits from the shared character catalog, plus the available sex options; or choose a distinct party of one to four saved characters. Character cards on the road open an overview of attributes, derived values, traits, and notes. The journey tracks **Supply**, **Wealth**, and **Reputation** separately.

Choose **Begin the journey** for the staged adventure or **Sandbox adventure** to explore a configurable ASCII map with the arrow keys or on-screen cardinal-direction buttons. In sandbox mode, `#`, `~`, and `^` are blocked by default; change the map, start coordinates, blocked symbols, and legend in the configuration.

During a session, choose **Quit to menu** to save and return later, delete the saved session, or keep playing. A saved session appears on the opening menu as **Resume saved session**; it is not automatically resumed when the game launches.

Game content and balance live in the **`game_data`** folder; edit the JSON file for the area you want to change—no Python changes are needed:

- `journey.json`: travel-day stage count, starting resources, wilderness and town upkeep, combat/event chances, settlement names and levels, and independently configurable minimum/maximum travel distances between towns and waystations.
- `combat.json`: attack damage, constitution mitigation, guard mitigation, and minimum damage.
- `sandbox.json`: map rows, start coordinates, blocked terrain, legend, and movement text.
- `monsters.json`: add, remove, rename, or rebalance monsters using `name`, `description`, `health`, `chance_to_hit` (an integer percentage from 0 to 100), `damage` (base damage on a hit), and `defense` (the target number for party attacks).
- `events.json`: add or rewrite events and their choices. Each choice has a `label`, `result`, and integer `supply`, `wealth`, and `reputation` changes. Events support one to four choices and require a positive integer `rarity` weight; higher weights make an event more likely when an event roll succeeds. `journey.json`'s `event_chance` sets the per-travel-day event probability separately from `combat_chance`.
- `text.json`: change the opening copy, journey and combat descriptions, button labels, endings, and journal messages. Keep the existing `{placeholders}` in formatted messages.

The game checks the configuration at startup and reports invalid values or missing files/fields. `config.py` loads and validates the separate configuration files. Python code is grouped by responsibility: `main.py` runs the game and dispatches input, while `rendering.py`, `creator.py`, `inventory.py`, `journey.py`, `combat.py`, and `persistence.py` contain the corresponding UI and gameplay systems. `shared.py` holds common constants and helpers.

## Rules, equipment, and inventory

Open **Inventory** from the road or a character overview to browse the rules catalog. The party shares Wealth and a common stash; each character also has a personal inventory with Strength-based carrying capacity (encumbrance begins above Strength, and carrying more than twice Strength is not allowed). Items can be bought for Wealth, moved between the stash and characters, and weapons or armor can be equipped. Spell knowledge belongs to individual characters; learning is free, while casting spends Magica.

Journeys begin in a town and then alternate between waystations and towns. Each travel day consumes the configured wilderness Supply cost; the distance to each next stop is randomly generated from `journey.distance`. When Supply runs out, travel remains possible but each unsupplied day costs every living character `journey.starvation_per_day` points, draining Stamina first and applying any remainder to Health (default 10). At zero Supply, choose **Forage for supplies** to make Wisdom checks (Survivalists succeed automatically; a Fishing Kit grants advantage). Towns sell equipment and charge the configured Wealth upkeep only when the party chooses **Rest in town**; shopping and resupply do not incur that upkeep. Towns also repair damaged armor for 1 Wealth, restoring up to the town's level in AP per transaction; armor can only be repaired where its highest equipped piece's AP is at or below the town level. Town trade access depends on settlement level plus party Reputation; towns refuse resupply at Reputation -3 or lower. Both towns and waystations exchange 1 Wealth for `max(1, supply_per_wealth_base + Reputation)` Supply, and waystations never refuse resupply for low Reputation.

Combat is round-based: choose one move for every living party member, choose a target for attacks or spells, then lock the plan. Encounters roll against `journey.ambush_chance` (default 0.2): enemies get a free opening turn on an ambush; otherwise the party acts first. The party's moves resolve together before the enemies take their turn. Each monster has a fixed `chance_to_hit`; the targeted character rolls d100 to defend, with their best Strength/Agility modifier adjusting the hit chance by 5 percentage points per modifier point. Guard grants defense-roll advantage, and encumbrance imposes disadvantage. Monster `damage` is the base damage dealt on a hit, before Constitution, guard, and shield mitigation. Set each character's Front or Back row on their party card; switching rows in combat uses that character's turn. A melee attack against a Back-row character provokes opportunity attacks from living Front-row characters. Weapon damage is rolled using the equipped weapon's damage dice; attribute bonuses require the matching Martial Training rank (1 for non-advanced melee, 2 for non-advanced ranged, 3 for advanced melee, and 4 for advanced ranged), while rank 5 adds +2 to attack rolls. Characters without Shei ancestry or Sorcery rank 5 have a maximum spell capacity of zero; eligible characters can know up to their Willpower modifier in spells, and Shei gain one additional spell slot from their Magical Bloodline. Human characters have a `62 + (2 × level)` point budget for Ambitious; other ancestries use `60 + (2 × level)`. The creator includes selected traits in that budget, and the character sheet shows spell capacity and recorded ancestry features. Outside combat, cast learned ally-target spells from Inventory and select a party member as the target.

The bottom journal shows five wrapped lines at a time; scroll over it or use its arrows to browse the full journey log. Slower combat animations show the attacker and target, then display a clear HIT or MISS indicator; successful non-attack spells display CAST. Attack, defense, damage, recovery, repair, foraging, encounter, and ambush rolls are recorded with their results and applicable target numbers. The included catalog draws from the supplied rules references: armor/helmets/shields provide breakable Armor Points, and guard improves defense. Encumbered characters roll checks with disadvantage. Spells provide a selection of combat healing, protection, control, and damage effects. Choose each character's downtime activity on their party card: recover 1d6 Health, 2d4 Magica, or 2d6 Stamina, or repair expended AP. AP repair rolls a d6 per point and succeeds only on a result greater than the armor's base AP; a failed point is permanently broken until professional repair. Sewing Kits and Smith's Tools grant the rules' d8 repair die for light and medium/heavy armor, respectively; Simple Construction grants +1 and Master Crafter grants +5 to repair rolls.

The game opens in a larger resizable window and scales its layout to fit the available space. Hoverable controls and valid spell targets highlight and use a hand cursor. Party cards keep each character's weapon, armor, helmet, and shield visible; Inventory labels these equipment slots explicitly and supports moving items directly between characters or through the shared party stash.

Edit **`rules_catalog.json`** to change the available weapons, armor, adventuring gear, and spells. Each item has a stable `id` and display `name`; Wealth costs for gear are Hiraeth's configurable defaults, while a spell's `cost` is its casting cost in Magica. Weapon entries also define damage dice, damage type, range, attack attribute, and reference traits. Armor entries define their kind, AP, inventory slots, and traits. Spell entries define school, target, effect summary, and Magica cost. Existing save files are upgraded with empty inventories and default equipment when resumed.
